# Section-by-section playbook

Condensed from Lynda Schenk's (Purple Giraffe founder) instructional comments
embedded in the master MAP template, plus her recorded walkthrough of the
process. Read `map-structure.md` first for the overall shape — this file is
the "how, with what, and why" for each section.

## Industry research (feeds About → Industry research and insights, and Appendix 1)

**Content required**: relevant industry rules/regulations; industry size and
growth, market share, competition, customer needs, regulatory environment,
economic trends; relevant events/occurrences/trends that changed how
businesses or consumers behave; possible future direction and its effect on
the industry; accreditation requirements or standards.

**PESTE framework**: Political, Economic, Social, Technology, Environment.

**Resources**: printed industry material, websites/blogs/official-body
digital resources, media/industry-event resources, client conversations,
consumer surveys, existing research. **In practice**: some clients already
have a business plan — feed that in directly, it's faster and more accurate.
Where there isn't much industry data available, don't force it — use
competitor information instead and note the gap.

**Time**: historically 2–3+ hours of manual research; AI-assisted research
now does in seconds/minutes what used to take hours — use it, but verify
findings are real and current.

**In the document**: summarise for the client in About; put full findings/
citations in Appendix 1.

## Competitive summary (top 3 competitors)

**Process**: the client identifies their competitors — never invent them.
Review each competitor's whole public marketing footprint: website, social
media, any findable advertising activity/targeting, annual reports if
public.

**Complete these tables** (then reference in Appendix 2): Competitor Brand
Analysis, Competitor Social Media Review, Competitor Website Analysis.
Optional extra appendix material: competitor-website/client-conversation
notes, competitor social pages, customer/retail sites relevant to sales
channels, SEMrush's competitor analysis (SEO strategies, paid search,
social activity).

**Write the Competitive summary** as client-facing prose from those tables.

## SWOT analysis

Do this **after** research is complete — by this point the SWOT should
already be evident from what's been gathered, not a fresh research task.
Strengths/Weaknesses = internal, controllable, identified from the
customer's point of view where possible. Opportunities/Threats = external,
beyond direct control — focus on how to capture/mitigate them. No actions
box required here (SWOT feeds the audit sections, not its own action list).

## Key performance indicators

Every desired result needs a **timeframe** and must be **achievable** — not
aspirational fantasy numbers. If a suggested KPI (e.g. NPS) isn't something
the client will actually measure, drop that row rather than leave it
unmeasured.

## Marketing investment (conditional — ongoing PG engagement only)

Only include when the client is continuing with Purple Giraffe on retainer.
The "outsource marketing" line covers the PG retainer fee; add estimated
budgets for other out-of-pocket costs (website build, logo/brand work, paid
ad spend, events) per line item. Delete the whole section for audit-only or
one-off strategy engagements — this is the one section of a MAP where PG's
own commercial involvement can legitimately appear, and only in this
specific, scoped way.

## Target market

Detail every identified customer segment: age, price sensitivity, other
relevant attributes. Call out underutilised or over-targeted segments
explicitly. Repeat the 6-segment wheel for each distinct target market (copy/
paste the diagram). Optional persona ("Meet [Name]") avatars can be added
for extra colour, particularly when there's more than one segment — write
fresh, client-specific personas; never reuse another client's example
personas verbatim.

## Branding and positioning

This section covers logo, tagline, positioning, mission, vision, values,
USP — the **Audit** is what currently exists; the **Strategy** is the desired
future state; the gap between them becomes Actions.

- **Logo**: insert current logo image, check it has everything needed
  (social lock-ups, mono version, reversed version).
- **Brand association prism**: what the client *wishes* their brand to be in
  2–5 years — aspirational, not current-state. Actions come from the gap.
- **Positioning (Who/Where/What/Why/How)**: Who = core business/who they
  are; Where = based/sells; What = product or service; Why = why they're in
  business, why customers choose them; How = how they operate, their values.
- **USP**: one single sentence on what the company is best at; plus the
  brand driver platform (why they exist); plus 2–3 dot points on what the
  business stands for and why consumers choose them.
- **Point of difference**: 3–10 points — can include length of operation,
  location, people, skills, product strengths, pricing, size, growth
  potential, assets, ethos, brand values, reputation, history, production
  facilities/techniques, qualifications/accreditations.

## Corporate assets

Covers letterhead, business cards, presentation templates, brochures,
invoices, quotes (add others as appropriate). Audit = current state and
summary of findings. Strategy = direction forward (e.g. "keep as-is" or
"move from paper quoting to digital"). Action = what's required.

## Physical experience / venue-specific topics

For clients with a physical premises: does it have signage, what's the
shopfront like, has it hosted events (what were they like), is there a
winery/venue (what's it like)? Compare briefly against competitor venues.
Resources: site visit or in-depth client conversation, competitor website/
venue visits.

## Traditional advertising

Review all assets/strategies in place, not limited to: print, radio scripts/
schedules, billboards/public graphic artwork, brochures, TV/video, Sensis
strategy, competitor activity, signage, direct mail, telemarketing,
guerrilla marketing, merchandise.

## Public relations / Influencers / Sponsorships / Events attendance

All conditional — delete if not applicable. PR: review current PR/media
strategy, the client's own news/media website section, past press releases
and media coverage gained. Influencers: past relationships, who, results/
success rate. Sponsorships: current events/memberships/associations.
Trade shows: previously attended events, past attendance reports.

## Customer service

Review quality and channels available (phone, email, chat, social). Cover
platforms/tech used, complaint resolution process, staffing/scheduling,
current feedback collection method.

## Website

**Points of focus**: obvious errors, first impression, accuracy of company
info, intuitiveness, brand feel. **Pages to audit**: main navigation +
footer pages, a sample search-results page, a landing page, a blog post, the
404 page — desktop and mobile separately, and cross-check any issue against
other browsers.

**Tools**: manual check for most items; **SEMrush** for meta data and the
starred (*) checklist items. Full page list and checklist criteria in
`map-structure.md`.

**Page speed**: Google PageSpeed Insights (free, pagespeed.web.dev) —
Core Web Vitals, performance/accessibility/best-practices/SEO scores, with
concrete recommendations. Use alongside SEMrush reporting.

**Image format**: PNG/JPEG are no longer the recommendation — website images
should be WebP or AVIF. Flag any non-WebP/AVIF images as an action.

## SEO content

Google's ranking priority is expertise/authority/trust — the SEO content
audit covers content quality and how well the site conveys that to Google.
**Primary focus**: keyword targeting, content quality, links. **Pages to
audit**: highest-trafficked pages/posts, highest-value pages, pages with
significant traffic drops. **Tool**: SEMrush Custom Reporting (Top Organic
Keywords screenshot, Organic Competitive Positioning Map). Checklist:
on-page keyword targeting (title, meta description, H1/H2s, image filenames/
alt text, not keyword-stuffed body copy), front-loaded/compelling meta
title+description within character limits, no broken images, organised/
accurate/thorough content, working internal links, backlink quantity/quality.

## GEO content

Newer addition (Generative Engine Optimisation — AI search visibility).
Checklist: feature case studies, authoritative content, client testimonials,
informative/optimised FAQs, alt text and meta descriptions present, content
optimised to directly answer questions, information consistent across
platforms, full service detail explained site-wide, good-quality/high-res
images, detailed/accurate/well-structured content. Also pull SEMrush Site
Audit data: site health %, crawled pages (healthy/broken/have issues),
redirects/blocked/errors/warnings/notices, thematic reports (crawlability,
HTTPS, international SEO, Core Web Vitals, site performance, internal
linking, markup), and top issue types (duplicate title tags, missing meta
description, low text-to-HTML ratio, underscores in URL, long title
element).

## Search engine marketing (SEM) — conditional, active-SEM clients only

Review past SEM/PPC budget, focus, results; existing keywords and ad copy;
landing page optimisation; ad performance metrics (targeting, demographics,
interests); competitor analysis via SEMrush. Resources: client conversation,
Google Ads login access.

## eNewsletter and email direct marketing (eDM) — conditional, active-EDM clients only

Review email list quality (active subscribers, bounce/unsubscribe rates),
segmentation strategy, design/content, deliverability and sender
reputation, regulation compliance, automation strategy, open/click-through/
conversion rates and revenue generated, opt-in forms. Resources: direct
access to the client's EDM platform (Mailchimp, Klaviyo, etc.) — most
clients don't actually look at their own data, so this often surfaces real
findings.

## Google Business Profile

Almost every business should have one — delete only for the rare exception.
Check: location correct, address details, hours of operation, reviews and
rating, updated images, posting at least monthly.

## Citation management

Check whether the business has current, consistent citations across
directory sites (Gumtree, Yellow Pages, etc.). Purple Giraffe maintains an
internal reference citation list — ask a team member for the current
version rather than rebuilding one from scratch. Use SEMrush's "List
Management" audit for an Online Presence rating and listing recommendations.

## Reviews

Audit across Google, social platforms, and industry-specific review sites:
profile accuracy/comprehensiveness, categories/services listed, image/post
uploads, reviews and interactions, audience insights. Build a KPI table by
location (current result / desired result / industry standard) and define
what review strategy is being implemented to grow reviews.

## Third-party online platforms — conditional, only if applicable

Trip Advisor, Expedia and similar, Airbnb, wedding platforms, local/tourism/
industry-association sites. Analyse platform selection, profile
completeness, content, and reviews.

## Social media marketing

See `social-media-checklists.md` for the exact per-platform checklist
(Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, YouTube). General rules:

- This section (platform review, content themes, current posting activity,
  tone, hashtags/emojis) is **audit only** — describe what currently exists,
  don't introduce strategy here. Strategy belongs in the later Marketing
  strategy sections (Social media objectives, Content pillars, Posting
  strategy, Tone of voice, Emojis, Hashtags).
- Resources: existing social handles/logins/passwords, past campaign
  reports, competitor handle research, client's stated appetite to invest in
  the channel, SEMrush Social Media Analysis, whether Reels are in use.
- Flag inconsistency between current content themes and the desired
  strategy as an action at the end of the audit section.
- Paid social media activity is conditional — delete if the client isn't
  running any; if they are, review past campaigns/results and pull SEMrush
  Advertising Research.
- Events advertising is conditional — only relevant if the client runs
  events (should they list on Facebook Events? Use free event listings?).
- Negative feedback management: if the client has no policy, the action is
  to develop one; if they already have one, just document it in the audit.

**Platform voice reference** (reusable boilerplate, adapt to the client):
Facebook combines text and images, preferred for broad reach and community
building. Instagram is visual-first — photos, graphics, short video.
Facebook/Instagram (Meta) reward consistent posting and follower engagement;
every post should have clear tone, a strong visual, and a CTA/link back.
X (Twitter) is for fast-moving thoughts/news, heavily used by media and
political accounts in Australia — good for quick reach on a current event.
LinkedIn ranks posts by personal connection, interest relevance and
engagement probability — tagging and replying boosts reach among a
professional audience. Add other platforms (YouTube, TikTok, Threads,
Pinterest) as relevant to the client.

## Analytics

Recommend a third-party consolidation dashboard (e.g. a Data Studio/Looker
Studio-style tool) connected via each platform's API, rather than reporting
from each platform individually — this is the efficient way to track
audience profiles, impressions, engagement and other metrics across
channels weekly.

## Images and video

Review current image/video library (if any), image quality, branding
consistency, CTAs, copy, and platform-specific sizing/optimisation.

## Formatting and delivery rules (apply to every MAP)

- Convert/write the whole document in **Arial**.
- Images: JPEG for photos, PNG for screenshots; keep each under ~500KB;
  consistent sizing throughout; don't crowd margins, the footer, or the PG
  giraffe mark; add captions where useful.
- Delete irrelevant sections **with tracked changes on** so removals are
  visible for review, not silently gone.
- Number appendices chronologically as the document is completed, and give
  each a clear title.
- **Before delivery**: every instructional (blue) comment and blue
  placeholder text must be actioned and removed — none should remain in a
  document going to a client. This is Purple Giraffe's own stated rule, not
  an inference — treat it as a hard gate, not a suggestion.
