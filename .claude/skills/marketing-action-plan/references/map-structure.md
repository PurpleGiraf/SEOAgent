# Marketing Action Plan (MAP) structure

Derived from two real, delivered Purple Giraffe MAPs: a lean single-entity commercial
plan (Duco Limited, IT managed services, March 2025) and a complex multi-brand
public-sector plan (History Trust of South Australia, April 2026). Use this as the
canonical shape — adapt the conditional sections to the client, never invent new
top-level structure.

## The repeating unit (used everywhere in "Marketing audit, strategy and actions")

Every channel/topic in the audit section follows this exact sequence. Never skip a
step; never merge two steps into one paragraph.

1. **`[Topic]`** — `PGHeading2` (grey subheading)
2. **Audit** — `PGHeading3` label, then `PGBodycopy` paragraph(s) describing the
   *current state only*. Use a checklist/data table where the channel has measurable
   facts (e.g. site audit checklist, platform followers, KPI current-vs-desired-vs-
   industry-standard). Never blend recommendations into the Audit — it is observation,
   not opinion.
3. **Strategy** — `PGHeading3` label (only when the topic needs a strategic framing
   beyond direct actions — some short/simple topics go straight from Audit to Actions).
   `PGBodycopy` paragraph(s) stating the *recommended direction*, not a bullet list of
   tasks.
4. **`[Topic] actions`** — light-grey callout box (`PGAction` for the box's own bold
   intro if there is one, `PGActionbullets` for every bullet). This is the only place
   concrete, assignable tasks appear. Every action listed here must also appear in the
   Marketing and communication calendar of activities.

Sub-bullets (indented, prefixed `-`) are used inside an actions box when a single
action needs enumerated options (e.g. referral incentive types) — don't overuse this,
most actions are flat bullets.

## Full document skeleton

| # | Section | Always / Conditional | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| — | Cover page | Always | Client logo, "Marketing Action Plan", month/year |
| — | Table of Contents | Always | Auto-generated from headings |
| 1 | Purpose | Always | 3-4 paragraphs: what this plan does, primary objective, how it's structured |
| 2 | Business objective and commercial alignment | Conditional | Include when the client has explicit, named commercial priorities to align to (common for larger/institutional clients). Bullet list. |
| 3 | Marketing strategy on a page | Recommended | 3×3 grid table: Audience/Strategies/Activities columns × Target markets/Marketing goals/Marketing channels, Channels/Key strategies/Tactics, Value proposition/Pricing & positioning/Measures of success rows |
| 4 | Marketing strategy roadmap | Recommended | Q1–Q4 wave graphic (goal + 4-6 steps per quarter) — the executive-level version of the detailed calendar |
| 5 | About | Always | Sub-sections: `<Client> overview` (bullet facts about the business) + `Industry research and insights` (summary, footnoted, full detail deferred to Appendix 1) |
| 6 | Competitive summary | Always | 2-4 named competitors. Prefer the structured Strengths/Weaknesses bullet format (HTSA-style) over pure prose (Duco-style) — it's clearer and easier for the client to scan. Detailed data goes in Appendix 2. |
| 7 | SWOT analysis | Always | Standard 2×2 table + a "SWOT summary" prose paragraph beneath it |
| 8 | Key performance indicators | Always | Table: KPI / Current result / Desired result / Comments (add a "% Change" column when both current and desired are numeric). Group rows by category (e.g. Organisational/audience, Commercial/participation, Digital/channel) for larger plans; a flat list is fine for simple ones. |
| 9 | Target market(s) | Always | One 6-segment wheel diagram per target market (Demographic/Geographic/Psychographic/Behavioural/Social-graphic/Occasional), each with a short "I want..." quote and age/gender/income/education/life-stage bullets. Cite the source of segmentation research if one exists. |
| 9a | Audience personas | Conditional | "Meet [Name]" narrative persona pages — use for institutional/complex clients, skip for lean commercial plans |
| 10 | ESG factors | Conditional | Public sector / values-driven clients only |
| 11 | Public value and impact-led marketing | Conditional | Public sector / not-for-profit only |
| 12 | Accessibility | Conditional | Public-facing physical/digital venue clients, government clients |
| 13 | Diversity, equity and inclusion | Conditional | Public sector, larger organisations |
| 14 | Products/services portfolio and pricing | Always | What the client sells, current pricing/packaging notes, and any pricing-guideline action items |
| 15 | Sales channels and distribution / Lead generation | Always | Table: Sales channel / Current % of sales / Desired % / Comments |
| 16 | Customer journey | Always | 5-stage funnel table: Awareness → Findability → Reputation → Conversion → Advocacy (or client-appropriate equivalent), each column listing current touchpoints, then an "Areas to concentrate on and grow" row |
| 17 | Audience data capture and nurture | Conditional | Include when the client has meaningful CRM/email capture gaps worth its own section rather than folding into eDM |
| 18 | **Marketing audit, strategy and actions** | Always | The core section — see repeating unit above. Sub-topics to include depend on the client (see topic list below) |
| 19 | Digital media | Always | Same repeating unit, digital-specific topics (see list below) |
| 20 | Marketing and communication calendar of activities | Always | Table: Marketing Element / Activity / Who / Qtr1 / Qtr2 / Qtr3 / Qtr4, shaded cells marking the quarter(s) an activity runs in. Every action box bullet from the whole document must appear here. "Please note, Q1 refers to [months]..." framing note above the table. |
| 21 | Appendix 1: Industry research | Always | Full cited industry/sector research (rules & regulations, market size/growth, trends, regulatory environment, technology evolution) — footnoted with real, checkable sources |
| 22 | Appendix 2: Competitor analysis | Always | Competitor review, pricing (if available), website analysis (traffic volume/sources/SEO keywords/bounce rate table), social media analysis |
| 23 | Appendix 3: Sales channel/revenue data | Conditional | Only when raw revenue-by-channel data was supplied and is too granular for the main Lead generation section |

## Topic list for "Marketing audit, strategy and actions" (non-digital)

Branding and positioning (+ Brand association prism, Positioning Who/Where/What/Why/How
table, Mission, Vision, Values and Culture, USP, Point of difference) → Brand assets/
Corporate assets → Experience structure and presentation → Industry awards → Tourism
marketing (if applicable) → Commissionable experiences (if applicable) → Physical
experience → Building/venue activation (if applicable) → Stock/retail management (if
applicable) → Loyalty program strategy (if applicable) → Traditional advertising →
Public relations (PR) → Influencer marketing (if applicable) → Sponsorships and
associations → Business/consumer events and trade show attendance → Customer service →
Partner-distributed promotion → Stakeholder and community outreach → Internal advocacy
→ Cross-portfolio promotional mechanisms (multi-brand clients only) → Referral program
strategy

## Topic list for "Digital media"

Websites (site audit checklist table, per-page desktop + mobile audit tables, image
optimisation review, analytics snapshot) → SEO content (checklist table + site health/
crawled pages/thematic reports/top issues tables) → Search engine marketing (SEM) →
eNewsletter and email direct marketing (eDM) (open rate/CTR/bounce/unsubscribe vs.
desired vs. industry-standard table) → Google Business Profile → Citation management
(directory-by-directory listing status) → Reviews (count/rating vs. desired vs.
industry-standard table) → Third-party online platforms → Social media marketing
(platform-by-platform: followers, post frequency, content themes, current posting
activity, tone of voice, hashtags/emojis, engagement, objectives table, content
pillars, posting strategy) → Images and video → Analytics (traffic overview,
geographic breakdown, engagement, sessions by source, tech/browser breakdown, summary)

## What NOT to do

- Don't invent metrics, traffic numbers, or competitor data — if it wasn't supplied or
  found through the client's own analytics/GSC/GA4/SEO audit tooling, mark it
  "Baseline to be established" or "TBC", exactly as both real MAPs do throughout.
- Don't include every conditional section for every client — a small commercial
  client does not need ESG/DEI/Accessibility/Public value sections; a government/
  cultural-sector client usually does.
- Don't recommend a channel or service Purple Giraffe doesn't actually offer — check
  `service-context.md`.
- Don't let an action appear in a callout box but not in the final calendar, or vice
  versa — they must match exactly.
